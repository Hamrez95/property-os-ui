## 1. PESTEL

### Political / institutional
- real-estate registration is becoming more electronically integrated
- official/private-platform access is permissioned and can change
- government data interoperability can create both opportunity and dependency

Strategic response:
keep regulated capabilities adapter-based and gated.

### Economic
- high inflation
- weak/volatile growth
- purchasing-power pressure
- nominal costs reprice quickly

Strategic response:
low fixed burn, frequent pricing review, ROI/loss-prevention positioning.

### Social
- property is financially and emotionally important
- trust often remains person-to-person and document-based
- multi-party relationships are common: owner, tenant, manager, family, representative

Strategic response:
role-aware UX, explainable trust, collaboration.

### Technological
- high internet/mobile reach
- strong habit of online property discovery
- fragmented operational tooling
- government/private APIs may expand

Strategic response:
mobile-first core, PWA/web access, API-first backend, evidence/provenance.

### Environmental
- building energy/water/maintenance and resilience matter over time
- environmental/energy data can later become property intelligence

Strategic response:
do not over-scope R1; keep Asset/Maintenance model extensible.

### Legal
- property rights, apartment common/private areas, leases and official registrations require careful boundaries
- electronic registration rules continue to evolve

Strategic response:
legal review, official references, no misleading "verified/official" labels.

## 2. VRIO-style resource review

### Domain model
Value: High
Rarity: Medium initially
Imitability: Medium
Organization: being built
Conclusion: temporary advantage unless converted into data/history/workflows.

### Property history graph
Value: High
Rarity: grows over time
Imitability: difficult after years of history
Organization: requires retention and data governance
Conclusion: potential durable moat.

### Trust/evidence graph
Value: High if users value verified records
Rarity: Medium–High
Imitability: operationally expensive
Organization: requires inspection/provider quality
Conclusion: potential moat after scale.

### Relationship graph
Value: High
Rarity: Medium
Imitability: network/history dependent
Conclusion: useful switching cost and acquisition loop.

### Brand trust
Value: Critical
Rarity: earned
Imitability: hard if reputation compounds
Conclusion: must be protected by security/privacy quality.

## 3. Porter implications

High rivalry + high substitute pressure means:
- "more features" is not strategy
- repeated workflow value is mandatory
- free/simple alternatives set usability benchmark
- distribution loops matter as much as product completeness

---

## 4. Ansoff-style growth sequence

### Market penetration
Same core product, same urban owner/manager segment.
Priority: R1.

### Product development
Trust, inspections, owner intelligence.
Priority: R2/R5.

### Market development
New cities / professional property managers.
After repeatability.

### Diversification
Short-stay operations or regulated transaction rail.
Only after strategic gates.
