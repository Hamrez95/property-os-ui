## 0. Executive thesis

Property OS should **not** enter the market as "another real-estate listing app."

The defensible wedge is:

> **The operating record for a property before, during and after any listing or transaction.**

A property can exist in Property OS for years without ever being listed. That creates a fundamentally different retention model from classified marketplaces.

The strategic sequence is:

```text
Own the property record
→ own recurring operations
→ earn trust/evidence
→ enable high-intent market activity
→ connect regulated transaction rails
→ compound proprietary property history
```

This sequence should guide product scope, hiring, marketing, monetization and fundraising.

## 1. Vision, mission and strategic intent

### Vision — 7–10 year direction

Make every important property in Iran understandable, manageable and progressively verifiable through one persistent digital operating record.

### Mission — next 3 years

Help owners, tenants and building managers run the day-to-day life of property with less ambiguity, lost information and fragmented communication—then use that verified history to make listing and transaction workflows safer.

### Strategic intent — next 18 months

Prove that users will repeatedly return to Property OS **before** they need to buy or sell.

If recurring property operations do not create retention, the larger transaction vision becomes a thin marketplace feature set.

## 2. Strategic problem map

Iranian property workflows are fragmented across:
- messaging apps
- paper contracts and scanned files
- phone calls
- spreadsheets
- building-manager notebooks
- bank transfers
- classifieds
- separate short-stay platforms
- official systems
- personal memory

The key problem is not simply "finding a property."

It is that **identity, rights, relationships, documents, maintenance, financial obligations, trust and transaction context are disconnected.**

### Jobs-to-be-done

#### Tenant / Resident
"When I live in a property, help me know what I owe, what expires, what is broken, what the building manager announced, and where my contract/history is."

#### Single-property owner
"Help me keep my property, tenant, documents, maintenance and lease under control without learning property-management software."

#### Multi-property owner / investor
"Give me one portfolio view for occupancy, expiry, cash flow, maintenance and risk."

#### Building manager
"Help me manage residents, charges, expenses, announcements, maintenance and governance transparently."

#### Buyer / renter
"Help me understand what is claimed, what is evidenced and what is verified before I commit time or money."

#### Seller / landlord
"Turn my existing property record into a trustworthy listing without re-entering everything."

#### Inspector / service provider
"Give me scoped work, evidence capture, auditable delivery and reputation."

## 3. Market thesis

### Evidence
- Iran had about **92.4 million people in 2025** in World Bank data.
- Digital 2026 estimates **73.8 million internet users** and **79.6% internet penetration** in late 2025.
- Roughly **78% of the population was urban** in the latest digital-demographic profile.
- The Iranian property market already has strong online discovery habits through large classified platforms and specialized property/search products.
- Current official registration rules are moving more real-estate workflows toward electronic identity, verification and registration interfaces, including access paths for licensed private platforms under regulation.

### Interpretation
The opportunity is not to teach Iranian users to "use the internet for property." That behavior exists.

The opportunity is to move them from:
**search / chat / paper / memory**
to
**persistent operating record / evidence / workflow / trusted projection.**

### Constraint
Macroeconomic volatility is severe. World Bank 2025 data reports negative GDP growth and high inflation; 2026 CPI releases show further acceleration.

Implications:
- pricing must be frequently revisited
- long fixed-price annual B2C plans are risky without indexation logic
- users are highly price-sensitive
- "saving money / preventing loss / avoiding missed obligations" is stronger messaging than abstract productivity
- payroll and vendor costs need scenario buffers

Strategic value: best initial paid B2C/B2Prosumer segment

### Segment C — Multi-property owner
Frequency: high  
Willingness to pay: medium–high  
Retention driver: portfolio + financial + occupancy + team access  
Strategic value: high LTV; strong SaaS economics

### Segment D — Building manager / board
Frequency: high  
Willingness to pay: medium, often shared across units  
Retention driver: charges + announcements + maintenance + governance  
Strategic value: distribution wedge because one manager can activate many households

### Segment E — Property professional / agent
Frequency: high  
Willingness to pay: medium–high if workflow saves time  
Strategic value: later channel and transaction distribution
Risk: can pull product into listing-first behavior too early

### Segment F — Inspector / service provider
Marketplace-side supply  
Strategic value: trust layer, service revenue, evidence creation

### Segment G — Short-stay host
Frequency: high but separate use case  
Strategic value: later expansion for villas; should not distract R1

## 5. Recommended beachhead

### Primary beachhead
**Urban owner / resident + small building manager**

Reason:
1. recurrent pain exists without a property transaction
2. users already possess the data/documents needed to start
3. manager invitations can create multi-user activation
4. recurring events create retention
5. trust history accumulates naturally
6. the product can later generate a Listing from the same record

### Avoid as first beachhead
- pure buyer search
- mass marketplace
- official transaction platform
- full short-stay OTA
- large-enterprise property management

Those are larger but require network effects, regulated integrations, supply density or heavier sales.

## 9. Monetization architecture

### Principle
Charge for **ongoing value or high-trust actions**, not for merely storing a property.

### Suggested packaging hypothesis

#### Free
- 1 active property relationship
- core property record
- basic documents
- basic reminders
- resident/building participation

Goal: activation + collaboration + network loops.

#### Owner Plus
- multiple properties
- advanced reminders
- finance/lease history
- richer documents/history
- export
- role/representative controls
- owner intelligence later

#### Building
- unit/resident roster
- charge scheme
- debts/payments
- expenses
- announcements
- maintenance
- governance
- audit/export

Price based on building size bands, not per manager seat only.

#### Trust / Inspection
Pay-per-use:
- inspection request
- verified evidence package
- verified passport refresh

#### Professional later
- higher property limits
- team seats
- listing/prospect workflow
- API/export where justified

### Pricing rule in high inflation
Keep product price architecture stable but review nominal IRR/Toman prices frequently.
Do not promise multi-year fixed nominal pricing.

## 11. Growth model

### Primary loop — Property collaboration loop

Owner creates Property
→ invites tenant / family / representative / manager
→ second user joins existing Property
→ operational data becomes more complete
→ reminders/messages/charges create return usage
→ trust/history improves
→ users invite additional stakeholders

This is more defensible than paid acquisition alone.

### Secondary loop — Building loop

Manager creates Building
→ adds units
→ invites owners/residents
→ charges/announcements require repeated visits
→ residents add property/lease/service data
→ more buildings become referable

### Trust loop

Property history
→ evidence
→ inspection/verification
→ stronger verified passport
→ higher-confidence listing
→ buyer/renter engagement
→ more incentive to maintain records

## 15. North Star Metric

Candidate:

> **Monthly Active Managed Properties (MAMP)**

A Property counts only if it had a meaningful management event in the month:
- lease
- document/evidence
- role/invitation
- building charge
- maintenance
- inspection
- listing/offer
- verified update

Why:
- avoids vanity registered-user counts
- ties product value to the core resource
- works before marketplace launch
- supports owner/tenant/manager roles

Supporting quality metric:
**Collaborative Property Rate** = % of active properties with 2+ active parties.

## 17. Competitive strategy

### Classified marketplaces
Strength: audience, liquidity, habit  
Weakness for our thesis: transaction/listing-centric; limited persistent operating record.

### Building-management apps
Strength: focused recurring workflow  
Weakness: narrow scope; property identity/history often not portable.

### Traditional agencies
Strength: local trust/network and negotiation  
Weakness: fragmented data, manual handoff.

### Official systems
Strength: authority  
Weakness: not designed as daily property operating UX.

### Property OS strategic position
Connect these worlds without trying to replace all of them immediately.

## 19. Porter-style industry forces

### Existing rivalry — High
Classifieds, property portals, building apps and offline incumbents.

### Buyer switching cost — Low at first
Must create switching cost through history, collaboration and stored evidence—not lock-in tricks.

### Supplier power — Medium
Inspectors, SMS/payment providers and later official integration partners can affect cost/availability.

### New entrants — Medium
UI can be copied; trusted history and operational network are harder.

### Substitutes — Very High
WhatsApp/Telegram, Excel, paper, bank transfers, Divar, traditional manager notebook.

Strategic consequence:
The product must be materially easier than "do nothing + chat."

## 21. Economic scenario planning

### Scenario A — Stabilization
- lower inflation
- better consumer planning
- annual subscriptions easier
- transaction volume may recover

Action:
accelerate premium owner/building plans and marketplace testing.

### Scenario B — High inflation / weak growth
- user price sensitivity
- salary/vendor repricing
- property used as store-of-value
- long-term nominal prices become stale

Action:
focus on cost avoidance, portfolio control, frequent pricing review, low fixed burn.

### Scenario C — Regulatory acceleration
Private licensed platforms gain practical access to official transaction APIs.

Action:
prepare adapter architecture, partner/legal readiness, do not build unlicensed money custody.

### Scenario D — Integration restriction
Official access remains limited or slow.

Action:
Property OS remains valuable as operational/trust layer; external official step becomes documented handoff.
